### Summary Assessment
This paper presents ACC, a conditionally-gated controller for a 10-DOF wheeled biped that combines PD balance, a proximity-gated position integral with asymmetric envelope follower, scheduled pitch stiffness, gated damping boost, flight recovery, and per-leg terrain adaptation into a two-channel torque assembly. The empirical evaluation is unusually thorough for a single-controller paper: 8-direction push sweeps, factorial additive/subtractive ablation, 4 LQR variants including a coupled 6-state 3D formulation, 46-configuration gain sweep, noise/delay robustness matrix, gate sensitivity analysis, and honest statistical power discussion. The measured idle precision (0.73 mm CoM RMS) and push survival (F_min = 80 N, F_med = 110 N) are impressive for a pure classical controller.

However, the paper has significant domain-positioning weaknesses. The core mechanisms--conditional integration, gain scheduling, envelope detection--are well-known control techniques presented under new branding ("Anchored Conditionally-Gated Control"). The paper acknowledges connections to selector/override control (Astrom & Hagglund, 2006) and conditional-integration AW in Appendix A, but the main text's novelty claims are stronger than the technical contribution warrants. The absence of any formal stability analysis for a controller with state-dependent switching is a meaningful gap, even for an empirically-oriented robotics venue. Three bibliography entries (Di Carlo 2018, Ferreira 2021, Kim 2017) are never cited in the text. The platform comparison table presents ACC favorably in every row and marks competing platforms as "---" for capabilities they have demonstrated. The sim-to-real transfer claim remains entirely hypothetical. These issues are addressable through reframing, additional references, and tempered claims, hence Major Revision rather than Reject.

### Detailed Comments

#### Literature Review
- **Coverage**: The paper covers the major wheeled biped platforms (Ascento, Handle, DIABLO, Whleaper, SKATER, Swiss-Mile) and relevant control paradigms (LQR, WBC, MPC, RL). The disturbance rejection coverage (DOB, ADRC, capture point, flight control) is adequate. However, the switched/hybrid systems literature is entirely absent (see W7), as is the broader literature on multi-mode/multi-primitive controllers in legged robotics (e.g., the MIT Cheetah multi-mode controller work). The Ollie platform (Ferreira 2021) is included in the bibliography but never discussed; given that Ollie is explicitly a wheeled inverted pendulum education platform with relevant balance control insights, this is a missed opportunity.
- **Integration quality**: The related work section is structured thematically (platforms, then disturbance rejection) but remains largely enumerative. There is limited critical synthesis -- e.g., the paper does not explain WHY prior controllers did not achieve simultaneous sub-millimeter precision and push recovery, beyond noting that it is a "trade-off." A deeper synthesis of what specific design choices in prior work prevented this combination would strengthen the research gap argument.
- **Research gap argument**: The gap (no classical controller achieves simultaneous precision standing AND push recovery AND terrain adaptation) is reasonably well-motivated. However, the paper does not adequately establish that this gap is fundamental rather than an artifact of prior work not targeting these specific metrics.

#### Theoretical Framework
- **Appropriateness**: The paper operates within classical control theory (PD control, integral action, anti-windup, gain scheduling, envelope detection). This is appropriate for the problem. However, the paper does not engage with the theoretical framework that would be most natural for its contribution: switched/hybrid dynamical systems theory (see W2, W7).
- **Application depth**: The framework application is empirical rather than analytical. Each control component is defined by its equation and validated through simulation. There is no derivation from first principles, no linearization analysis, and no formal justification for why the specific gate thresholds (e.g., 0.05-0.15 m for g_prox, 0.25-0.50 m/s for g_env) are optimal beyond empirical tuning. The sensitivity analysis partially addresses this by showing insensitivity to some parameters, but does not replace analytical justification.
- **Alternative frameworks**: A sliding-mode control formulation could achieve similar regime-dependent behavior with formal robustness guarantees. A model-predictive control formulation with terminal constraints could provide the "anchor" behavior with stability certificates. These alternatives are not discussed.

#### Academic Argument Quality
- **Factual accuracy**: The claim that "no single classical controller integrates all three [precision, push recovery, terrain]" (Introduction, line 61) is difficult to verify. WBC frameworks (e.g., the one cited from Klemm 2020) can in principle handle all three if properly tuned. I cannot confirm this claim is false, but it is stated as fact without qualification. The Zamani et al. (2020) reference is described as "a coupled 3D LQR... demonstrating coupled pitch-roll stabilization on a platform without articulated legs" -- I note this reference appears to be from ICRA 2020; the paper should verify the exact venue and author list, as I cannot independently confirm this reference's details.
- **Argument logic**: The argument that "LQR baselines all fail because of the PID servo path, not controller structure" is well-supported by the direct-torque variant's improved (but still failing) performance and the coupled 6-state LQR's identical performance to the 4-state variant. The logic is sound, though the conclusion that the PID servo path is "the" bottleneck rather than "a" bottleneck could be softened.
- **Terminology precision**: "Anchored" and "conditionally-gated" are not standard terms in the control literature. "Anchor" appears to mean "precision holding at a reference position" -- the control-theoretic term would be "regulation" or "setpoint tracking with integral action." "Conditionally-gated" is a descriptive term for what the hybrid systems literature calls "state-dependent switching" or "mode-dependent activation." While coining terminology is acceptable when it aids clarity, the paper should explicitly map its terms to standard control-theoretic vocabulary to avoid the appearance of claiming novelty through rebranding.

#### Contribution to the Field
- **Incremental contribution**: The primary contribution is empirical: demonstrating that a carefully tuned combination of conditional integration, asymmetric envelope detection, scheduled stiffness, and gated damping achieves simultaneous sub-millimeter precision and omnidirectional push recovery on a 10-DOF wheeled biped. This is a valuable engineering contribution -- it provides a recipe and parameter tuning methodology that other practitioners can follow. The specific technical novelties are the asymmetric envelope follower applied to quiet-stance detection and the systematic gate parameter tuning methodology.
- **Positioning**: The paper positions itself as a new control architecture. A more accurate positioning would be: a principled integration of established control techniques with careful attention to the interaction between conditional integration gating and envelope-based mode detection, validated through thorough empirical ablation.
- **Overclaiming**: Several claims risk overstatement: (a) "no single classical controller integrates all three" -- as noted above, WBC frameworks could in principle do so; (b) "reduced sim-to-real transfer gap" -- entirely unsubstantiated; (c) the "two-channel" framing as architectural novelty; (d) Appendix A's claim that ACC has "no direct AW analogue" -- while the specific coupling of state-gated integral with envelope-detected damping is specific to ACC, the individual components all have AW analogues.

### Questions for Authors

1. Why was the LQR cost matrix Q,R selection limited to Bryson's rule? A systematic LQR tuning (e.g., grid search over Q,R diagonal elements, or LMI-based regional pole placement) might yield a stabilizing LQR through the PID servo path. Can the authors report what happens when the LQR is tuned specifically for robustness to the PID servo lag (e.g., by adding a Pad\'e approximation of the delay to the linearized plant model)?

2. The paper acknowledges threshold sensitivity of the anchor mechanism to sensor noise -- at 10x consumer IMU noise, ACC falls immediately. Given that the EMA is the gating signal, would a Kalman filter pre-processing stage (fusing IMU + odometry) reduce the noise floor enough to make the EMA-based gating viable on hardware, or is the fundamental issue that the EMA cannot distinguish broadband sensor noise from physical motion regardless of filtering?

3. The 5-step tuning recipe for porting ACC to new morphologies relies on identifying the dominant ringdown period T_r. How robust is this recipe to changes in mass distribution (e.g., adding a payload)? The paper tests mass/damping perturbations only qualitatively. Would the authors expect the release time constant to scale with sqrt(m) as for a linear oscillator, or is the relationship more complex due to the coupled pitch-height dynamics?

4. The gate parameter sensitivity analysis shows the pitch stability gate g_theta has near-zero sensitivity. Is this gate effectively redundant, or does it prevent a specific failure mode that the factorial ablation (S5) revealed but the sensitivity analysis missed? Clarifying this apparent contradiction would aid reproducibility.
